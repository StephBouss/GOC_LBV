Assala Energy: a decisive milestone

The acquisition of Assala Energy's assets aims to strengthen energy sovereignty and reposition the State.

June 2024·2 min read

The negotiations that led to the acquisition of Assala Energy's assets are among the most significant undertakings for Gabon's energy future. Completed in June 2024, this purchase by the Gabonese State, through Gabon Oil Company (GOC), was part of a clear strategy: to bring more of the upstream oil sector under national control, strengthen energy sovereignty and put the State back at the centre of resource governance. However, integrating the country's second-largest producer has proved complex, and recent news has been mixed, alternating between operational progress and major technical challenges.

On the one hand, Assala's production has recovered markedly. In December 2025, a 26.6% increase brought output to 57,000 barrels per day, a direct result of investment in the mature Rabi field, one of the historic pillars of Gabon's oil industry. This performance shows the ability of the State and GOC to revive mature assets, optimise existing operations and consolidate the value generated by national resources.

At the same time, however, the subsidiary has faced significant setbacks in exploration. In early 2025, three wells (Rabunga-A, Rossignol-A, Totou-NE) turned out to be dry, and another was abandoned. These failures, common in the industry but strategically sensitive for a country relaunching its oil sector, have affected Assala's ability to offset the natural decline of mature fields. They also raise questions about the exploration strategy, the quality of the geological portfolio and, above all, the transparency of management, as the figures were published late, fuelling concern among partners and industry observers.

At a time when Gabon is seeking to stabilise its oil industry to finance economic diversification, Assala plays a central role. The company must now show that it can not only sustain the performance of its mature assets but also deliver on its exploration projects. GOC, for its part, will need to turn this acquisition into a real driver of growth, innovation and energy sovereignty.
